What I learned building this

Exit codes lie; logs don't.

The single most "real" decision here — letting a tool exit 0 and still FAIL on log content — is the one that made it feel like actual EDA infrastructure instead of a toy task runner.

Threads are underrated.

Everyone reaches for asyncio. But when your workers live inside subprocess.wait(), the GIL is already released — a plain ThreadPoolExecutor gave real parallelism for a fraction of the complexity.

A status enum is a design.

The whole product lives or dies on WAIVED and SKIPPED being distinct from PASS and FAIL. Getting those four states right — and how they flow through the diff — was 80% of the actual thinking.

Killing a process is harder than starting one.

A shell wrapper dying while its child simulator lives on is a classic infra bug. Process groups, a grace period, then a hard kill — and making that portable across POSIX and Windows — took more care than the happy path.

Scope discipline is a feature.

No plugins, no async, no web server in the core. Saying no to good ideas kept it small enough to actually read — which, for a study project, was the entire point.
